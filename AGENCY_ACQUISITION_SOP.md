> **Don't sell what we know how to build. Find what they already hate doing, then build the smallest thing that removes it.**

# Agency Acquisition SOP v1

## 1. Objective

**Goal:** Find and convert marketing agencies into customers for AI/automation services.

Initial commercial target:

> **$10k/month revenue from agency automation services.**

The first milestone is not $10k.

It's:

> **1 paying customer → repeatable delivery → 3 customers → predictable acquisition → scale.**

---

## 2. ICP

### Primary

**Digital/marketing agencies**

Prefer:

* 10–49 employees
* Established client base
* Recurring client work
* Multiple service lines
* Already spending money on software/tools
* Operational complexity
* Evidence of manual/repetitive workflows
* Ability to pay ~$500+ for an initial project

### Why agencies?

Because they already understand:

**clients → revenue → operations → tools → outsourcing.**

We're not trying to convince them that businesses need services.

---

## 3. Prospect Source

Primary source:

**Clutch**

Why?

Because it gives us:

* Agency category
* Employee count
* Reviews
* Services
* Client feedback
* Project information
* Evidence of operational patterns

We're using Clutch as a **problem-discovery database**, not merely an email list.

---

## 4. Prospect Selection

We don't blindly take every agency.

For each candidate ask:

### A. Is it our ICP?

10–49 people?

Recurring agency work?

Multiple clients?

### B. Is there operational complexity?

Look for:

* SEO
* PPC
* content
* social
* web
* reporting
* account management
* multiple specialists
* multiple client accounts

### C. Is there a pain signal?

This is the important part.

Look through reviews for things like:

* communication problems
* account-manager transitions
* reporting issues
* project coordination
* client requests
* delays
* repetitive work
* handoffs
* scaling problems
* data scattered across systems

**We prefer observed evidence over assumptions.**

---

## 5. Build a Hypothesis

Before contacting them, write:

> **Hypothesis:** [specific workflow] may contain repetitive/manual work.

Example:

> When an account moves between strategists, transferring client context may require manual work.

Not:

> "They need AI."

That's bullshit unless we've established the underlying problem.

---

## 6. Find the Right Person

Hierarchy:

### 1. Operations / Client Delivery

Best for operational workflow problems.

### 2. Account/Client Strategy

Best for client-management and handoff problems.

### 3. Performance/Marketing Operations

Best for campaign/reporting workflows.

### 4. Founder/CEO

Use when the problem is company-wide or no better operational contact exists.

We don't automatically email founders just because they're easy to find.

---

## 7. Verify Contact

This is now a **hard rule**:

> **Never invent an email address.**

Verification hierarchy:

**Best:**

Official company website → published email.

Then:

Official team/contact page → person + company contact route.

Then:

LinkedIn → current role.

Then other reputable public sources if necessary.

If we can't verify the route:

**SKIP.**

We do not spend 30 minutes fighting one prospect.

---

## 8. Write the Outreach

The email has five components:

### 1. Personal relevance

> "I came across X while researching..."

### 2. Show we understand their operation

Mention something actually relevant to their business.

### 3. One hypothesis

Not five.

### 4. One diagnostic question

The question is the payload.

### 5. Remove sales pressure

Something like:

> "I'm researching where agencies still lose time to repetitive operational work. Not pitching anything yet..."

This is intentional.

We're trying to start a **problem conversation**, not force a sales call from a stranger.

---

## 9. The Message Structure

Our current template:

> Hi [Name],
>
> I came across [Company] while researching how growing agencies handle the operational side of recurring client work.
>
> [Specific observation about their operation.]
>
> I'm curious: **[specific workflow question]?**
>
> I'm researching where agencies still lose time to repetitive operational work. Not pitching anything yet, just trying to understand where the actual bottlenecks are.
>
> Best,  
> Benjamin

The template stays relatively stable.

**The hypothesis changes.**

---

## 10. What Happens After Sending

This is crucial.

### If they don't respond

Don't immediately assume:

> "The offer sucks."

We don't yet know whether the problem was:

* subject
* timing
* person
* email deliverability
* hypothesis
* message
* company fit

We need enough observations before changing everything.

### If they reply with a problem

**STOP OUTREACH.**

Bring me the exact response.

Then we investigate:

**Problem → workflow → frequency → cost → current solution → desired outcome.**

---

## 11. If They Say "We Already Have a Tool"

That's not necessarily rejection.

Ask:

> What does the current system handle well, and where does the team still have to work around it manually?

Because we're not really selling:

**"AI."**

We're selling:

> **Removal of a painful manual workflow.**

If they already have software, there may still be gaps.

---

## 12. If They Confirm Pain

Then we move from research to sales.

### Discovery

Determine:

* **Frequency** — How often?
* **People** — How many employees touch it?
* **Time** — How long does it take?
* **Cost** — What's the approximate labour cost?
* **Consequence** — What happens when it goes wrong?
* **Current solution** — What do they use today?
* **Urgency** — Why fix it now?

The ideal problem becomes:

> Frequent + expensive + annoying + measurable + currently manual.

That's our sweet spot.

---

## 13. Then We Build the Automation

Only after the workflow is understood.

Potential stack:

**n8n + Supabase + APIs + LLMs + existing agency tools**

But the technology is downstream.

We don't start with:

> "What cool AI thing can I build?"

We start with:

> **"What repetitive workflow is costing this company money?"**

Then build the smallest intervention that removes it.

## 15. What We're Actually Learning

We're simultaneously learning **three things**:

### Business
Can you sell an automation outcome?

### Sales
Can you consistently turn cold strangers into conversations?

### Product
Which agency workflows are painful enough that companies will pay to remove them?

That means every rejection and every reply is **market information**.

---

## 16. What We Are NOT Doing

For this sprint:

* ❌ Building Atlas endlessly before selling
* ❌ Sending generic "AI automation" pitches
* ❌ Mass-emailing random companies
* ❌ Guessing emails
* ❌ Spending an hour researching one agency
* ❌ Building automations before understanding the workflow
* ❌ Changing the offer after three emails
* ❌ Chasing every shiny online business model
* ❌ Trying to become a content creator first
* **❌ Treating outreach as the product.**

Outreach is simply the instrument we're using to discover and sell the service.

---

## 17. Today's Actual Experiment

We've now sent several messages across different hypotheses.

That means we're entering the next stage:

### **Signal collection**

We're watching for:

* **Which hypothesis gets replies?**
* **Which problems get acknowledged?**
* **Which problems have economic weight?**
* **Which people respond?**
* **Which agencies already have sophisticated systems?**
* **Which workflows repeatedly appear?**

Eventually we'll stop hypothesising and start seeing a pattern.

That's when the offer gets sharper.
